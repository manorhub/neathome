This guide is for kitchens and bathrooms where the cabinet under the sink is dark, deep, and hard to use. Plumbing, garbage disposals, and cleaning supplies compete for the same space, so items get pushed to the back and forgotten.

You will learn how to work around plumbing, separate cleaning supplies from daily items, and keep the space safe and reachable.

## Work Around Plumbing First

Measure around pipes, not just the cabinet opening. Note the height of the trap, the position of supply lines, and how far the door opens. Any organizer must leave clearance for plumbing and for your hand to reach items without moving everything else.


## Separate Cleaning Supplies

Keep cleaning supplies in a contained bin or caddy so a leak or spill does not spread across the cabinet. Keep them away from food storage in kitchens, and keep medicines and personal items separate in bathrooms.

## Keep Daily Items in Front

Put the items you use every day in front, at a height you can reach without crouching into the cabinet. Put backup stock behind or above, only if you can still reach it safely.


## FAQ

### Can I use adhesive organizers under a sink?
Check the surface, weight limit, and moisture resistance first. Under sinks can be damp, so follow the product instructions and your lease rules.

### What if my under-sink cabinet is very small?
Use the door, the side walls, and a small pull-out or stackable option only if it clears plumbing. A smaller system you can maintain beats a large system you cannot reach.

### How do I handle leaks?
Check for leaks before organizing, fix or report a leak first, and do not store paper, cardboard, or items that would be ruined by water in a cabinet with a known leak.

This guide is for narrow gaps between a refrigerator and a wall, beside a washer, or next to a bathroom vanity. A slim rolling cart can turn wasted gap space into useful storage, but only if it fits, rolls safely, and does not block airflow or access.

You will learn how to measure a gap correctly, choose a cart height and depth, and use it without creating a new clutter problem.

## Measure the Gap, Not Just the Cart

Measure width at the top, middle, and bottom of the gap, since walls and appliances are not always straight. Measure height under any overhang, and depth from the front to the back wall. Leave a small clearance so the cart rolls in and out without scraping.

## Check Airflow and Access

Do not block refrigerator vents, washer access panels, or plumbing shut-off valves with a cart. Keep emergency access clear, and do not store flammable items near heat sources.

## Load Light and Balanced

Put heavier items low and lighter items high. Do not overload a slim cart, especially on wheels, and test that it rolls straight and stops safely before filling it completely.

## FAQ

### Can a slim cart fit between a fridge and a wall?
Only if the gap width, height, and depth all allow clearance on both sides. Measure at several points, not just at the front.

### What is best stored on a slim cart?
Small, frequently used items that fit the cart tiers, such as cleaning supplies, pantry extras, or bathroom supplies, depending on the room and safety rules for that space.

### How do I keep a rolling cart from becoming clutter?
Assign one job to the cart, label tiers if helpful, and remove items that do not belong in that gap during a regular clean-up.
